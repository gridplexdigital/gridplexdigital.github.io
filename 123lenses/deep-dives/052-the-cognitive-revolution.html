The Cognitive Revolution and Myth-Making

Approximately 70,000 years ago, Homo sapiens underwent a sudden genetic and linguistic mutation that transformed an insignificant African ape into the terror of the planet. Our evolutionary advantage was not brute strength or fire, but language capable of transmitting information about things that do not exist: fiction, myths, and gods.

In 1939, deep inside the dark, limestone chambers of the Stadel Cave in the Swabian Jura mountains of southwestern Germany, archaeological excavators uncovered hundreds of shattered, calcified fragments of mammoth ivory. It was not until thirty years later, when the fragments were meticulously cleaned and assembled like a three-dimensional jigsaw puzzle, that the breath of prehistory stunned the scientific world: the fragments formed a foot-tall, breathtakingly polished figurine of a being that has never walked the earth—a creature with the upright torso, arms, and legs of a human being, crowned by the muscular, snarling head of a Pleistocene cave lion. Carbon-dated to approximately 40,000 years before the present, the Lion-Man of Hohlenstein-Stadel (Löwenmensch) is the oldest undisputed figurative sculpture in human history. Experimental archaeologists using flint stone tools have calculated that carving this single mammoth ivory statuette required at least four hundred hours of continuous, skilled labor. Think about what that means: during the absolute depths of the last European Ice Age, when small bands of humans lived on the razor's edge of hypothermia and starvation, a band of hunter-gatherers agreed to feed, shelter, and protect an artist for months, exempting him from the life-or-death duties of tracking reindeer and foraging berries, solely so he could carve an imaginary creature out of a tusk! Why would natural selection favor a creature that wastes precious biological energy manufacturing toys of non-existent monsters? Because that lion-headed figurine was the physical birth certificate of the Cognitive Revolution. It was the material proof that human language had crossed an evolutionary rubicon: we had learned to communicate not merely about the physical world of trees, rivers, and leopards, but about the invisible, transcendent world of fiction, mythology, and shared spirits. In inventing the lie that could be collectively believed, an unremarkable African ape unlocked the secret to planetary conquest.

1. The Tree of Knowledge Mutation: Sapiens' Secret Weapon

In Sapiens, Yuval Noah Harari opens his macro-historical epic with a humbling biological truth: for the first 130,000 years of our existence, Homo sapiens was an animal of no significance. We were merely one of several human species (including Homo neanderthalensis in Europe, Homo erectus in Asia, and Homo floresiensis in Indonesia) inhabiting a marginal corner of East Africa.

We had big brains, opposable thumbs, and fire, yet we lived in the middle of the food chain, hunting small rodents, gathering roots, and cowering in caves from sabre-toothed cats. When Sapiens first ventured into the Middle East 100,000 years ago, they encountered robust Neanderthals and were soundly driven back into Africa.

Then, between approximately 70,000 and 30,000 years ago, something miraculous occurred: the Cognitive Revolution. New ways of thinking and communicating emerged, likely driven by accidental genetic mutations that rewired the internal neural architecture of the Sapiens brain—what Harari poetically terms the "Tree of Knowledge" mutation.

What was so revolutionary about Sapiens’ new language? It was not the ability to transmit information about physical danger. A green monkey can emit a specific vocal screech that warns its troop: "Look out! An eagle from above!" and a completely different screech that signals: "Look out! A leopard in the grass!" Bees perform complex waggle dances to communicate the precise direction and distance of a nectar field.

Sapiens’ linguistic breakthrough was threefold:

The Three Tiers of the Sapiens Linguistic Revolution

  1. The Descriptive Tier: The ability to transmit vast quantities of complex information about the surrounding physical environment: not just "There is a lion," but "This morning, near the bend in the river, I tracked three male lions stalking a herd of bison."
  2. The Social Gossip Tier: The ability to transmit information about human relationships: "Who hates whom? Who is sleeping with whom? Who is honest, and who is a liar?" (As Robin Dunbar proved, social gossip is the glue that coordinates primate cohesion).
  3. The Fictional / Mythological Tier: The unprecedented, miraculous capacity to speak about things that do not exist in the physical universe at all: "The lion is the guardian spirit of our tribe. If we paint its image on our chests and dance around the fire, the spirit will make our spears strike true."

Fiction did not merely give us campfire poetry; fiction gave us the ability to cooperate flexibly in large numbers.

2. Robin Dunbar: The 150 Limit and the Fiction Barrier

To understand why fiction is the foundation of human power, one must examine the work of Oxford evolutionary anthropologist Robin Dunbar.

In his landmark 1996 book, Grooming, Gossip, and the Evolution of Language, Dunbar analyzed the relationship between neocortex size and social group size across all primate species. Primates maintain group cohesion through physical social grooming—picking fleas, massaging fur, and touching. Grooming builds trust, forms alliances, and resolves conflicts.

However, physical grooming is biologically expensive: an individual chimpanzee can groom only one other chimpanzee at a time. Therefore, chimpanzee bands are strictly capped by neurobiology: once a troop exceeds fifty individuals, intimate grooming networks break down, factions form, and the troop inevitably fractures in a violent civil war.

Language allowed Sapiens to replace physical grooming with vocal grooming (gossip). You can gossip with four people simultaneously while eating, gathering roots, or cleaning a spear. Dunbar calculated that based on the human neocortex ratio, the maximum number of individuals that a human can maintain an intimate, stable, personal relationship with—knowing who they are, how they are related, and whether they can be trusted—is approximately 150 individuals (The famous Dunbar's Number).

Even today, Dunbar's Number remains an iron organizational constraint: military combat companies typically consist of around 150 soldiers; startups thrive without formal HR departments until they cross 150 employees; and traditional agrarian villages rarely exceeded 150 residents.

How, then, did Sapiens bridge the chasm from bands of 150 foragers to empires of fifty million citizens, multinational corporations of two hundred thousand employees, and religions of two billion believers?

"You could never convince a chimpanzee to give you a banana by promising him limitless bananas after death in chimpanzee heaven... But Sapiens can. Because any large-scale human cooperation—whether a modern state, a medieval church, an ancient city, or an archaic tribe—is rooted in common myths that exist only in people's collective imagination."
— Yuval Noah Harari, Sapiens (2014)

3. Sapiens vs. Neanderthals: The Triumph of the Mythological Coalition

The brutal empirical test of the Cognitive Revolution was the encounter between Homo sapiens and Neanderthals in Eurasia between 45,000 and 30,000 years ago.

In a purely physical, one-on-one cage match, a Neanderthal would have torn a Sapiens limb from limb. Neanderthals possessed denser bone structures, heavier musculature, broader chests, and braincases that were actually larger than modern humans. They had inhabited the frigid tundra of Europe for more than 300,000 years, possessed sophisticated Mousterian flint spearheads, and had mastered fire.

Yet within a few thousand years of Sapiens stepping out of the Levant and into Europe, Neanderthals were completely driven to extinction.

Why? Because Neanderthals lacked the Cognitive Revolution. Archaeological evidence indicates that Neanderthals lived in small, isolated family bands rarely exceeding fifteen to twenty individuals. They had sophisticated vocal communication about physical objects, but they produced no art, no symbolic jewelry, and no mythical figurines like the Stadel Lion-Man. Without myths, two separate bands of Neanderthals who met in a snowy valley viewed each other with immediate, lethal territorial suspicion. They could not form coalitions.

Sapiens, by contrast, carried the Stadel Lion-Man. A band of twenty Sapiens encountering another band of twenty Sapiens did not see strangers; they saw fellow children of the Great Lion Spirit. When war erupted over hunting territories, twenty Neanderthals did not face twenty Sapiens; they faced five hundred Sapiens coordinated under a single shared myth. Numbers crushed brawn.

4. The Papal Bull of Tordesillas: Dividing the Earth with a Myth

The sheer, mind-bending power of myth-making to organize millions of human bodies across planetary distances was demonstrated on June 7, 1494, in the small Castilian town of Tordesillas.

Two European maritime kingdoms—Spain and Portugal—were on the verge of a world war over the newly discovered navigation routes across the Atlantic and around the tip of Africa. To resolve the dispute, ambassadors from King Ferdinand and Queen Isabella of Spain sat down with ambassadors from King John II of Portugal.

They produced a treaty: The Treaty of Tordesillas. The diplomats took a map of the world and drew a single vertical meridian line from pole to pole, 370 leagues west of the Cape Verde islands (approximately 46 degrees west longitude).

The Treaty of Tordesillas Partition (1494)

The treaty declared that every ocean, island, continent, and mountain range west of that meridian belonged exclusively to the Kingdom of Spain; every land east of that meridian belonged exclusively to the Kingdom of Portugal.

With a few strokes of a quill on sheepskin parchment, two tiny European kingdoms divided the entire planet Earth—including billions of acres of unexplored South American rainforests, African coasts, and Asian archipelagoes inhabited by millions of indigenous people who had never heard of Europe!

What gave that sheepskin parchment the power to command armadas, send thousands of conquistadors across oceans, and reshape the genetic and linguistic map of the Americas?

The parchment had zero physical power. It had power because it was ratified by a Papal Bull issued by Pope Alexander VI in Rome. And why did anyone care what Pope Alexander VI said? Because millions of Catholic Europeans shared the exact same cosmic myth: they believed that the universe had been created by an invisible deity, that this deity had sent his son to be crucified in Judea, that this son had handed the keys of the kingdom to Saint Peter, and that Saint Peter's apostolic successor in Rome held divine authority to divide the globe!

Because millions of sailors, merchants, soldiers, and kings believed in that shared fiction, armies marched, navies sailed, and Brazil became Portuguese while Mexico became Spanish. Myth was the operating system of empire.

5. The Two-Edged Sword: Fictions as Weapons of Domination

Myth-making is the source of all human greatness—it built the pyramids, invented science, wrote constitutional republics, and created universal human rights. But Harari reminds us of its terrifying dark side: myths can just as easily be engineered to justify atrocities, slavery, and genocide.

When the Spanish conquistador Hernán Cortés landed in Mexico in 1519, he did not conquer the Aztec Empire through superior weaponry alone; he conquered it because he deployed a superior, ruthless intersubjective software: the legal fiction of the Holy Roman Empire and the Christian crusade.

In modern times, the most catastrophic tragedies occur when human beings forget that their fictions are creations of their own minds, and begin worshiping the fictions as absolute gods:

  • Fascism elevated the fiction of the "Aryan Volk" or the "Sovereign Nation" into an absolute deity, demanding that millions of living, breathing human beings be exterminated in gas chambers to purify an imaginary abstraction.
  • Stalinism elevated the fiction of the "Dialectical Inevitability of History" and the "Proletariat" into a secular religion, liquidating millions of peasants in the Gulags to serve an imaginary utopian timetable.

The ultimate test of wisdom in the post-Cognitive Revolution era is the ability to use myths to cooperate, while retaining the moral lucidity to remember that real suffering belongs only to living, sentient beings. A corporation does not feel pain; a nation cannot weep; a dollar bill cannot bleed. Only conscious creatures suffer. When we sacrifice living human beings on the altar of corporate brands or nationalist fictions, we have become the slaves of our own mythology.

6. Core Heuristics for the Myth-Making Animal

1. The "Suffering Litmus Test" for True Reality

Whenever you want to know whether an entity is an objective reality or a human fiction, apply Yuval Noah Harari’s Suffering Test: "Can this entity suffer?"

  • Can the Commonwealth of Massachusetts suffer? No. If its government collapses, land and trees remain; only human citizens suffer.
  • Can Microsoft or Apple suffer? No. If they go bankrupt, a legal charter is shredded; only employees and shareholders feel pain.
  • Can a dog, a horse, a soldier, or a child suffer? Yes! They feel visceral fear, hunger, physical agony, and grief.

Always subordinate the needs of imaginary entities (corporations, nations, ideologies) to the welfare of sentient beings that can actually suffer.

2. Master the Dunbar Boundary in Organization Design

If you are leading a team, department, or community, recognize the Dunbar Threshold: under 150 members, you can lead through personal relationships, informal trust, and open conversation. Above 150 members, informal trust breaks down. To scale an organization beyond 150 people, you must deliberately transition from informal rapport to formal, shared mythology: written core values, codified mission statements, clear organizational charts, and institutional rituals. If you don't build the myth, the tribe will fracture.

3. Recognize "Culture" as Evolutionary Software

In biology, evolutionary change requires hundreds of thousands of years of slow genetic mutation. A chimpanzee cannot decide to overhaul its troop governance from alpha-male hierarchy to a constitutional democracy next Tuesday. But Sapiens can! Because human behavior is governed by intersubjective myths (culture), Sapiens can change their entire behavioral architecture in twenty-four hours simply by rewriting the myth (e.g., the French Revolution overturning 1,000 years of divine monarchy in 1789). Culture is cognitive software that can be refactored.

4. Beware the "Naturalization of Myth" Trap

Every dominant culture claims that its social myths are not human inventions, but the eternal, unalterable laws of Nature or God (e.g., the divine right of kings, the caste system, or unconstrained free-market capitalism). Whenever someone tells you "This is just human nature; things have to be this way," remember the Stadel Lion-Man: we are the storytelling ape. What human imagination created, human imagination can rewrite.

7. Self-Diagnostic Checklist: Auditing Your Mythological Diet

  • What are the foundational stories and myths that govern my daily life (the American Dream, corporate success, religious dogmas, political parties)? Have I critically examined them, or did I inherit them unconsciously?
  • Am I currently sacrificing my physical health, mental peace, or family relationships for an imaginary corporate or ideological abstraction that cannot feel pain?
  • When leading my team, do I rely exclusively on administrative commands, or do I communicate a clear, inspiring narrative that helps people understand why our work matters?
  • How do I react when someone critiques or mocks one of my sacred cultural or national symbols? Does my ego react as if my physical body was attacked?
  • Can I look at people belonging to rival political or religious 'mythological tribes' and recognize that they are ordinary human beings operating under different cultural software?

Public goods and the free-rider problem

Some benefits are difficult to restrict to paying users, so individuals may prefer others to bear the cost. This creates challenges for funding and maintaining shared resources.

⚡ 2-Minute Field Note 📖 Looking for the full study? Read the 12-minute deep dive with book quotes →

The idea

Some benefits are difficult to restrict to paying users, so individuals may prefer others to bear the cost. This creates challenges for funding and maintaining shared resources. Treat this as a lens for examining a situation, not as a universal explanation. Context matters: ask what evidence supports the interpretation, what it leaves out and when the idea might not apply.

Put it to use

Identify who pays, who benefits and how contribution is encouraged. Look for credible collective rules rather than relying only on goodwill.

A QUESTION TO CARRY

Where do you benefit from a shared resource without contributing enough to sustain it?

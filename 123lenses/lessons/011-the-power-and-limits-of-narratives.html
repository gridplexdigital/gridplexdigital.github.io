The power and limits of narratives

Stories organise facts into causes, characters and consequences. They make complex events memorable, but can hide uncertainty and make coincidence look inevitable.

⚡ 2-Minute Field Note 📖 Looking for the full study? Read the 12-minute deep dive with book quotes →

The idea

Stories organise facts into causes, characters and consequences. They make complex events memorable, but can hide uncertainty and make coincidence look inevitable. Treat this as a lens for examining a situation, not as a universal explanation. Context matters: ask what evidence supports the interpretation, what it leaves out and when the idea might not apply.

Put it to use

When you hear a compelling story, separate the sequence of events from the evidence that connects them causally.

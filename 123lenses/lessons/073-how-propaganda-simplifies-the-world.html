How propaganda simplifies the world

Propaganda often uses repetition, emotional cues, selective facts and in-group/out-group narratives. Its influence depends on context, trust and distribution, not merely on false statements.

⚡ 2-Minute Field Note 📖 Looking for the full study? Read the 12-minute deep dive with book quotes →

The idea

Propaganda often uses repetition, emotional cues, selective facts and in-group/out-group narratives. Its influence depends on context, trust and distribution, not merely on false statements. Treat this as a lens for examining a situation, not as a universal explanation. Context matters: ask what evidence supports the interpretation, what it leaves out and when the idea might not apply.

Put it to use

Notice what a message asks you to fear, admire or resent. Check what it omits and whether it discourages independent verification.

A QUESTION TO CARRY

What emotion does a political or commercial message try to activate first?

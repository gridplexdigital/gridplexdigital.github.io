The luxury trap: why technological ease creates new burdens

Improvements in aggregate productivity often increase individual daily toil rather than leisure. Luxuries tend to become necessities and spawn new obligations.

⚡ 2-Minute Field Note 📖 Looking for the full study? Read the 12-minute deep dive with book quotes →

The idea

The Agricultural Revolution promised food security, but resulted in longer working hours, poorer nutrition, and epidemic diseases for the average peasant. Harari terms this the 'luxury trap': humanity adopts an innovation that makes life slightly easier, grows dependent on it, increases population to match the surplus, and ends up trapped in a higher-stress baseline.

Put it to use

Before adopting a time-saving convenience, ask whether it will genuinely grant you leisure or simply raise the expectation of higher daily volume.

A QUESTION TO CARRY

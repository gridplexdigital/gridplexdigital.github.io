Loss aversion and the fear of giving something up

Losses often feel more consequential than equivalent gains. This can make people cling to the familiar or reject worthwhile change.

⚡ 2-Minute Field Note 📖 Looking for the full study? Read the 12-minute deep dive with book quotes →

The idea

Losses often feel more consequential than equivalent gains. This can make people cling to the familiar or reject worthwhile change. Treat this as a lens for examining a situation, not as a universal explanation. Context matters: ask what evidence supports the interpretation, what it leaves out and when the idea might not apply.

Put it to use

Compare options by their full future consequences, not only by what you might lose relative to the status quo.

A QUESTION TO CARRY

What opportunity are you avoiding mainly because change feels like a loss?

The danger of human irrelevance in the automated age

The 20th century was a struggle against exploitation; the 21st-century danger is obsolescence. Being economically irrelevant is far more perilous than being exploited.

⚡ 2-Minute Field Note 📖 Looking for the full study? Read the 12-minute deep dive with book quotes →

The idea

In industrial economies, elites needed masses of workers to operate factories and soldiers to fight wars, forcing states to invest in universal education, health, and civil rights. In an artificial intelligence and automation economy, if millions of humans lose their economic utility, the social contract fractures unless reinvented around human dignity.

Put it to use

Cultivate rare, high-resonance human qualities—deep empathy, moral courage, systemic synthesis—that cannot be automated into software.

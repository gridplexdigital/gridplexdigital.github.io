The precautionary principle and unintended consequences

When potential harm is serious and evidence incomplete, precaution may be justified. But excessive precaution can also block beneficial action; proportionality matters.

⚡ 2-Minute Field Note 📖 Looking for the full study? Read the 12-minute deep dive with book quotes →

The idea

When potential harm is serious and evidence incomplete, precaution may be justified. But excessive precaution can also block beneficial action; proportionality matters. Treat this as a lens for examining a situation, not as a universal explanation. Context matters: ask what evidence supports the interpretation, what it leaves out and when the idea might not apply.

Put it to use

Compare the scale and reversibility of possible harms with the costs of delaying action. State what evidence would change the policy.

A QUESTION TO CARRY

How should a decision-maker act when both action and inaction carry risks?

LESSON 099 / 123 · Decision Science & Cognitive Biases

Why intelligent people make poor decisions

High IQ is an engine of processing speed and pattern recognition, not epistemic humility. When smart people err, they construct more formidable rationalizations for their mistakes.

⚡ 2-Minute Field Note 📖 Looking for the full study? Read the 12-minute deep dive with book quotes →

The Core Mental Model: Dysrationalia

Intelligence measures cognitive processing capacity (working memory, abstract logic, verbal fluency). Rationality measures whether your beliefs map to reality and whether your actions achieve your true goals. You can possess massive computational horsepower while running contaminated, dogmatic software.

Why High IQ Backfires

Higher intelligence does not produce more objective scrutiny. Instead, greater verbal and analytical speed enables smart thinkers to build airtight justifications for intuitions they have already emotionally adopted.

The Epistemic Trap

Confidence is a function of cognitive ease and narrative coherence, not truth. Articulate thinkers weave stories so seamlessly that they fool themselves long before they persuade others.

⚡ Tactical Heuristic: The Falsification Threshold

Before committing capital, prestige, or organizational effort to an initiative, write down: "What specific, observable evidence would prove this thesis wrong?" If you cannot specify a disconfirming condition, you are rationalizing an emotional attachment, not making a sound decision.

A QUESTION TO CARRY

Which cherished belief would you find hardest to abandon, and what evidence would genuinely force you to revise it?
